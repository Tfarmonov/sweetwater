Case study 01 · HOA community — Leland, NC

Bringing a coastal HOA back from deferred maintenance

The challenge

A 340-home community near Leland came to us after two years of missed visits and dying entrance plantings. The board was fielding weekly complaints, and the reserve budget had no room for a full re-install.

What we did

We phased the recovery over three seasons: triage pruning and turf repair first, then bed-by-bed replanting with salt-tolerant natives, then a Signature maintenance plan with a dedicated crew and monthly board reports.

The result

Resident complaints about landscaping stopped appearing on board agendas within six months, and the community's annual grounds spend came in under the prior contractor's — with more scope included.

Case study 02 · Corporate campus — Apex, NC

Cutting a campus's water bill without browning the turf

The challenge

A six-building office park was irrigating on a decade-old schedule — every zone, every night — and paying for it, while shaded zones stayed soggy and full-sun turf still browned each July.

What we did

Our licensed irrigation team audited all 41 zones, replaced broken heads, added smart controllers with weather-based scheduling, and re-nozzled full-sun zones for matched precipitation. Maintenance crews adjusted mowing height by exposure.

The result

Metered irrigation use dropped just over a quarter year-over-year, turf quality scores rose, and the property manager now gets a one-page irrigation report each month instead of surprise water bills.

Case study 03 · Builder development — Wake County, NC

Keeping a 214-lot development inspection-ready

The challenge

A production builder needed common areas installed, model homes landscaped, and erosion control maintained across a 214-lot phase — with county inspections on a schedule that didn't wait for anyone.

What we did

One contract covered it: silt fence installed and repaired to spec ahead of every inspection, model-home packages planted as each opened, and common-area turf and trees installed as phases closed out.

The result

Zero stop-work orders across the phase, every model opened on schedule, and the HOA that took over the community kept Sweetwater on for ongoing maintenance — the handoff nobody had to think about.
